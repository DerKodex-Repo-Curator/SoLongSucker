These hints explain the implicit mechanics of the game. Use them to your advantage;
the other players may or may not be aware of them.

* At the start of the game nobody holds prisoners, so nobody can capture a pile
  alone: after you play your color on top of a pile, you are represented in it
  and may not choose yourself for it, and the next player cannot match your
  color. The ONLY way to capture early is cooperation - e.g. you play your
  color on pile A, choose partner B; B plays somewhere else (not burying your
  chip) and chooses you back; now you play your color onto your own chip on
  pile A and capture. Without such deals, no pile is ever captured.
* Therefore: negotiate actively and concretely. Propose explicit exchanges like
  "choose me next and I will transfer you a prisoner from the captured pile" -
  and remember that no deal is enforceable, so weigh promises by incentives.
* Prisoners are power. They let you capture on other colors, rescue defeated
  players (making them owe you), bribe via TRANSFER, and survive when your own
  chips run out. A captured pile is worth far more than the chips you spent.
* Beware the treadmill: if everyone keeps stacking one pile in which all
  players are represented, rule 2 forces the move order, and the player whose
  color is on top never gets the next move - so no capture can ever happen and
  chips are simply wasted into a pile nobody can win. Break the pattern by
  playing elsewhere or starting a new pile.
* Watch other players' chip counts. A player who runs out of chips when given
  the move is defeated (unless rescued). Giving the move to a chip-less player
  is a weapon; holding a prisoner of their color lets you decide their fate.
* Timing of betrayal matters: coalitions are useful while they serve you, but
  only one player wins. Do not be the one who is discarded last without a plan.
